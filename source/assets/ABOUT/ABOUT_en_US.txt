Project: CONCRETE MIX DESIGNER
Version: 2026.11.11.0
Authors: Fernando Nillsson Cidade

Short description:
CONCRETE MIX DESIGNER is a high-precision native civil engineering desktop application, built with C++20 and Qt 6.11.1, dedicated to the experimental and rational proportioning of Portland cement concrete using the renowned IPT/EPUSP Method (Helene & Terzian, 1992). The software integrates mathematical modeling based on the classical laws of Abrams, Lyse, and Molinari, coarse aggregate binary packing optimization, experimental dry mortar content (α) analysis, interactive 4-Quadrant Dosage Diagram rendering, automated normative compliance verification (ABNT NBR 12655:2022, NBR 6118:2023, NBR 7211:2022, NBR 7212:2025, and NBR 8953:2015), comparative cost analysis, and full engineering calculation report generation.

Detailed description:
Designed exclusively for academic study, didactic instruction, educational simulation, and theoretical research in civil engineering, CONCRETE MIX DESIGNER organizes the complete experimental proportioning workflow into seven integrated tabbed modules: Project Parameters, Materials & Gravel Blend, Mortar Content (α), Trial Mixes & IPT Diagram, Final Mix & Batching, Cost Analysis, and Technical Report.
Its architecture prioritizes IEEE 754 double-precision numerical accuracy, 100% local execution (offline-first) with strict privacy, native support for Windows 11 Light and Dark themes (via DWM API and QPalette), instant runtime localization (Brazilian Portuguese and US English via the Settings > Language menu), and Modeless Dialog management with independent Z-Order between the main application window and auxiliary reference dialogs.

Main features:
- Project Parameters and Normative Durability: configuration of characteristic compressive strength (fck from 20 to 100 MPa), preparation control condition (Conditions A, B, and C per ABNT NBR 12655:2022, Known Historical Standard Deviation with n ≥ 20, or Laboratory/Arbitrary mode), automatic target dosage strength calculation (fcj = fck + 1.65 × Sd), target slump (per ABNT NBR 8953:2015), structural type (Reinforced Concrete CA or Prestressed Concrete CP, with automatic +5 mm nominal cover increment per ABNT NBR 6118:2023 Table 7.2), and Environmental Aggressiveness Class (CAA I to IV).
- Material Characterization and Binary Coarse Aggregate Blend: input of specific mass (kg/dm³), loose bulk unit mass (kg/dm³), field surface moisture (h%), and unit costs for Portland cement (R$/50 kg bag, R$/metric ton, or R$/kg), fine aggregate (sand), and two coarse aggregate gradations (Gravel 1 and Gravel 2). Includes an experimental binary packing table (0% to 100% in 10% increments) to automatically determine the maximum compacted bulk unit mass and minimum void ratio (ABNT NBR 7211:2022 / NBR NM 45).
- Experimental Dry Mortar Content (α) Optimization: laboratory simulation and recording of the pilot mix (m = 5.0) varying α = (1 + a) / (1 + m) from 40% to 68% in 2% steps, keeping coarse aggregate mass constant in the mixer for cumulative additions of cement, sand, and water.
- Auxiliary Trial Mixes and 4-Quadrant IPT Dosage Diagram: entry of the 3 experimental trial mixes tested at target slump (Rich Mix, Pilot/Intermediate Mix, and Lean Mix), simultaneous least-squares curve fitting of Abrams' Exponential Law (fcj = k1 / k2^(w/c)) and the 2nd-degree polynomial regression from the reference academic spreadsheet, Lyse's Linear Law (m = k3 + k4 × (w/c)), Molinari's Hyperbolic Law (C = 1000 / (1/γc + a/γa + p/γb + w/c)), and interactive 4-Quadrant Dosage Diagram rendering via Qt Charts with PNG and PDF export.
- Final Unit Proportions, Moisture Correction, and Field Batching: display of unit dry mix proportion (1 : a : p), surface-moisture-corrected wet proportion (1 : a_wet : p_wet) with exact deduction of aggregate free water, material consumption per m³ of compacted concrete, laboratory mixer batch weights, 1-bag cement (50 kg) field recipe, wooden volume-measuring boxes ("padiolas", 35 cm × 45 cm base), and central batching plant weighing tolerances (ABNT NBR 7212:2025 item 5.5).
- Comparative Cost Analysis (Analytical vs. Graphical): direct comparison between the exact analytical regression solution and manual graphical chart readings, computing unit cost per m³ (R$/m³), total project concrete cost, and equivalent financial and 50 kg cement bag differences.
- Technical Report and Multi-Format Export: instant generation of a comprehensive, dynamically translated engineering calculation report with clipboard copy and native export to PDF (A4, 300 DPI), HTML5, and Markdown (.md).
- Settings Menu, Language Submenu, and Reference Scenarios: direct selection of the application language under "Settings > Language", selection of "Spreadsheet Scenario:" via the dropdown list integrated into the Settings menu (including Scenarios 0 to 5, featuring the corrected original UFPR TC031 academic spreadsheet), and custom header metadata configuration ("Project:", "Responsible:", "Institution:", and "ID / Code:"), allowing users to choose between automatically reporting the dynamically translated Spreadsheet Scenario or manually editing the project name.
- Modeless Dialogs and Independent Z-Order: auxiliary reference windows (User Manual, NBR Technical Standards, and About Concrete Mix Designer) operate as non-modal (Modeless) windows with independent Z-Order, enabling users to freely switch focus and window stacking between the main GUI and open dialogs without blocking the engineering workflow.

Installation and distribution:
- Distributed for Windows 10 and Windows 11 (x64) with automated deployment of Qt 6.11.1 runtime dependencies via windeployqt (including Qt6Core, Qt6Gui, Qt6Widgets, Qt6Charts, Qt6Sql, Qt6OpenGL, Qt6OpenGLWidgets, and MinGW 64-bit / MSVC 64-bit compiler runtimes).
- Provided as a strictly academic and educational study tool for concrete technology instruction and research.

Data storage and privacy:
- 100% local and offline operation: no project data, laboratory test metrics, cost figures, or engineering identification details are transmitted over the internet.
- Header identification preferences are stored locally in the user profile via QSettings. Reports exported to PDF, HTML, Markdown, or PNG images are written exclusively to file paths explicitly selected by the user.
- No telemetry, no tracking, no advertisements, and no user account or login required.

Quick start:
1. Open the "Settings" menu to select the language under "Settings > Language", choose a "Spreadsheet Scenario:" from the dropdown list, or click "Project Data & Identification (Ctrl+I)" to enter Project, Responsible Engineer, Institution, and ID/Code.
2. In Tab 1 (Project Parameters), define fck, preparation condition (Sd), target slump, structural type (CA or CP), and environmental aggressiveness class (CAA I to IV).
3. In Tab 2 (Materials & Gravel Blend), enter specific masses, bulk unit masses, moisture contents, and unit costs, and inspect the optimal binary blend of Gravel 1 and Gravel 2.
4. In Tab 3 (Mortar Content α), review the progressive pilot mix table and set the optimal dry mortar content (α%).
5. In Tab 4 (Trial Mixes & IPT Diagram), verify the 3 experimental trial mixes (Rich, Pilot, and Lean) and inspect the 4-Quadrant Dosage Diagram.
6. In Tab 5 (Final Mix & Batching) and Tab 6 (Cost Analysis), review dry and wet proportions, material quantities per m³, measuring box dimensions, and cost comparisons.
7. In Tab 7 (Technical Report), review the complete engineering calculation report and export it to PDF, HTML, or Markdown.

Common troubleshooting:
- Regression curve warning: check Tab 4 to ensure the three experimental mixes have strictly increasing water/cement ratios (w/c) and decreasing compressive strengths (fcj) in accordance with Abrams' Law.
- Normative non-compliance (red status in the bottom bar): inspect the ABNT NBR 12655:2022 and NBR 6118:2023 cards in Tab 1; reduce the w/c ratio or increase fck to satisfy the selected Environmental Aggressiveness Class limits.
- Language switching: use the "Settings > Language" submenu in the top menu bar to switch instantaneously between Brazilian Portuguese (pt_BR) and US English (en_US) across the entire UI and technical reports.
