Development History and Rationale

Motivation:
CONCRETE MIX DESIGNER was born from the practical engineering need to replace academic and laboratory spreadsheets—often prone to broken formulas, circular references, and accidental cell overwrites—with a highly reliable, auditable native civil engineering platform strictly aligned with current Brazilian technical standards (ABNT NBR 12655:2022, NBR 6118:2023, NBR 7211:2022, NBR 7212:2025, and NBR 8953:2015). Its direct inspiration originated from experimental structural concrete proportioning studies conducted at the Department of Civil Construction of the Federal University of Paraná (UFPR — course TC031 Civil Construction Materials II), grounded in the classical IPT/EPUSP method established by Paulo Roberto do Lago Helene and Paulo Terzian (1992).

Design goals:
- Mathematical and Normative Accuracy: unify the exponential analytical model of Abrams' Law, the quadratic polynomial regression of laboratory spreadsheets, Lyse's Linear Law, and Molinari's Hyperbolic Law into a deterministic, unambiguous C++20 calculation engine.
- Didactic and Operational Clarity: structure the mix design workflow in the exact physical sequence performed in the laboratory—from structural specification and aggregate characterization to mixer mortar content optimization, 4-Quadrant Dosage Diagram plotting, field moisture correction, and cost evaluation.
- Native and Accessible Visual Experience: integrate native Windows 11 controls with automatic Light and Dark theme adaptation, Modeless auxiliary dialogs with independent Z-Order stacking, and instantaneous runtime localization (Brazilian Portuguese and US English via the Settings > Language menu).
- Privacy and Local Autonomy: ensure students, professors, and researchers can simulate and export educational calculation reports (PDF, HTML, Markdown, and PNG) 100% offline, without cloud dependencies or telemetry collection.

Development and evolution:
1. Conception and Algorithmic Audit: comprehensive dependency mapping of input cells and formula precedents across the enhanced UFPR experimental dosage spreadsheet, eliminating rounding discrepancies and validating every equation against ABNT standards for academic study purposes.
2. C++20 and Qt 6.11.1 Architecture: development of a decoupled core engine (`source/core`) for rheological calculations, aggregate packing optimization, least-squares regression, and normative compliance, paired with a modular graphical user interface (`source/gui`) featuring interactive vector charts via Qt Charts.
3. UI and Usability Refinement: integration of spreadsheet scenario selection and the language submenu ("Settings > Language") into the Settings menu, full customization of technical report header metadata, smooth numeric editing in spinboxes, dark-theme contrast enhancements in the User Manual, and full adoption of Modeless Dialogs with independent Z-Order management.
4. Dual-Toolchain Distribution Quality (MinGW and MSVC): standardization of build automation and runtime DLL deployment for distribution as an academic study tool and open-source publication on GitHub.

Expected impact:
Provide the academic civil engineering community, teaching laboratories of concrete technology, and higher education institutions with an academic study tool that facilitates the understanding of proportioning laws, illustrates Portland cement consumption optimization per cubic meter, and demonstrates normative durability criteria for concrete structures.
